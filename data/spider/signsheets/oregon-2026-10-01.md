# Oregon attractions, both directions -- 2026-10-01

Draft for the owner's review, not yet approved. Whether Oregon carries new
attraction billboards at all is the owner's call; see the law note below and
the review file.

Sixteen real roadside attractions in Oregon, one of them just across the
Columbia at Maryhill, each signed in every direction a leg passes it, 74
signs in all. Every `leg:` is written the way the driver reads the sign and
`at_mi` counts from that end; `tools/bake_billboards.py` mirrors the milepost
onto a leg stored the other way round and records which way the billboard
faces.

Oregon's Motorist Information Act (ORS 377.700 to 377.844) caps billboards
rather than banning them: no permit for a new outdoor advertising sign has
been issued since 2007 (ORS 377.725), and a new location needs an existing
permitted sign taken down or a banked relocation credit. There were about
1,700 permitted billboards, with about 700 credits banked, at the Oregon
Legislature's 2009 count (measure analysis, 2009 session). So real billboards line Oregon's
Interstates, in fixed number. Three places carry none on this sheet: the
Columbia River Gorge National Scenic Area, where the management rules bar new
billboards (OAR 350-081-0112), from the Sandy River to the Deschutes; US 101,
the Pacific Coast Scenic Byway; and the Volcanic Legacy Scenic Byway, US 97
from the California line to Klamath Falls and Highway 140 west of it, where
23 USC 131(s) bars new signs.

Mileposts come from projecting each attraction onto the legs' dense geometry,
read against each leg's interchange list. "Next exit" signs stand between the
attraction's exit and the exit before it, one to two miles out; "ahead" signs
about eight to twelve miles out, closer where callouts leave no other room.
Several through legs from Sacramento and San Francisco to Portland run US 97
and Highway 58 rather than I-5 over the Siskiyous, which is why Collier and
Salt Creek Falls carry signs on them and Ashland does not. Each sign sits at
least 2.2 miles from every other callout heard in the same direction and
every other sign on the west-coast sheets.
