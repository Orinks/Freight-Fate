# West Virginia attractions, both directions -- 2026-10-01

Draft for the owner's review, not yet approved.

Seven real West Virginia roadside attractions, each signed in every direction
a leg passes it where the callouts allow, 25 signs in all. Every `leg:` is
written the way the driver reads the sign and `at_mi` counts from that end;
`tools/bake_billboards.py` mirrors the milepost onto a leg stored the other
way round and records which way the billboard faces.

West Virginia permits billboards beside its state road system, Interstates
included, under the Division of Highways' outdoor advertising permits (West
Virginia Code chapter 17, article 22). Its national byways (the Historic
National Road, Midland Trail, Highland Scenic Highway, Coal Heritage Trail,
Washington Heritage Trail and Staunton-Parkersburg Turnpike) and its state
byways, among them the Northwestern Turnpike on US 50, carry none of these
signs; every one stands on an Interstate (WVDOT, Byways and Backways map;
FHWA). The Trans-Allegheny Lunatic Asylum at Weston already has a museum
callout on the I-79 legs, so it is not signed again.

Mileposts come from the legs' interchange lists. "Next exit" signs stand
between the attraction's exit and the exit before it; "ahead" signs about
three to ten miles out where the Kanawha valley's village callouts leave
room. Each sign sits at least 2.2 miles from every other callout heard in the
same direction, and from every sign on the 2026-10-01 sheets.

## West Virginia State Capitol, Charleston

Cass Gilbert's capitol, dedicated 1932, 292 feet to the top of its gilded dome,
the tallest building in the state and a few feet taller than the dome of the
United States Capitol; tours through the Culture Center (Wikipedia, "West
Virginia State Capitol"). Signed on every leg into Charleston.

## Tamarack, Beckley (I-64/I-77 Exit 45)

The state's showcase of West Virginia crafts at One Tamarack Park by the
I-64/I-77 interchange, opened 1996: wood, glass, textiles, pottery, metalwork
and food, with resident artist studios (Wikipedia, "Tamarack Marketplace").
The legs list no Exit 45 except Beckley to Charleston, and the Prosperity
village callout sits just before it, so most signs read "ahead".

## The Greenbrier bunker, White Sulphur Springs (I-64)

Beneath The Greenbrier resort, Project Greek Island, the emergency bunker built
for Congress in 1959 to 1962 and kept secret until the Washington Post reported
it in 1992; never used. Tours run daily, ages ten and up, reservations required
(Wikipedia, "Project Greek Island"; greenbrier.com). Eastbound only: westbound
the state line and the White Sulphur Springs callout leave no room inside West
Virginia.

### The Greenbrier (eastbound)
- treatment: billboard
- leg: charleston_wv_us -> richmond_va_us
- at_mi: 114.8
- spoken: Billboard: The Greenbrier, ahead. Under the resort is a Cold War bunker built for Congress and kept secret for thirty years. Congress never showed up.

## Lost World Caverns, Lewisburg (I-64 Exit 169)

Open year-round, a National Natural Landmark; its twenty-eight-foot War Club
stalagmite is where Bob Addis sat for nearly sixteen days in 1971 for a world

## Flatwoods Monster Museum, Sutton (I-79 Exit 62)

On September 12, 1952, children and adults in Flatwoods reported a ten-foot
creature with a glowing red face; the free museum in Sutton tells it, and
Braxton County put up five giant monster-shaped chairs (Wikipedia, "Flatwoods
monster"). Southbound from Pittsburgh and on US 19 the Sutton and Flatwoods
callouts crowd the exit, so those signs read "ahead".

## Coopers Rock State Forest (I-68 Exit 15)

12,747 acres over the Cheat River canyon, with the main overlook and stone
shelters built by the Civilian Conservation Corps from 1936 to 1942; the main
entrance is open April through December (Wikipedia, "Coopers Rock State

## Wheeling Suspension Bridge, Wheeling

Opened 1849, the longest suspension bridge in the world until 1851; closed to
vehicles in September 2019 for overweight traffic, with a four-thousand-pound
limit, and permanently closed to motor vehicles in February 2026, still open to
people on foot and bicycles (Wikipedia, "Wheeling Suspension Bridge"). Signed
on the two legs into Wheeling; the eastbound sign stands on the Ohio side.
