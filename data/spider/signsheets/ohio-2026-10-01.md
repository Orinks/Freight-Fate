# Ohio attractions, both directions -- 2026-10-01

Draft for the owner's review, not yet approved.

Ten real Ohio roadside attractions, each signed in every direction a leg
passes it, 34 signs in all. Every `leg:` is written the way the driver reads
the sign and `at_mi` counts from that end; `tools/bake_billboards.py` mirrors
the milepost onto a leg stored the other way round and records which way the
billboard faces.

Ohio permits billboards beside its Interstates and primary highways in
commercial and industrial areas under an ODOT permit (Ohio Revised Code
chapter 5516). Ohio's national byways (FHWA) are the Amish Country Byway,
the Historic National Road (US 40), the Hocking Hills Scenic Byway, the Lake
Erie Coastal Ohio Trail, the Ohio and Erie Canalway and the Ohio River Scenic
Byway; none of these signs stands on one. The Y-Bridge carries US 40, but its
signs stand on I-70. US 24 between Toledo and Defiance, along the Maumee
Valley Scenic Byway, is left unsigned.

Mileposts come from projecting each attraction onto the legs and reading the
legs' interchange lists. "Next exit" signs stand between the attraction's exit
and the exit before it, one to two miles out unless a callout is in the way;
"ahead" signs about six to twelve miles out. A sign about a city stands on the
legs into it. Each sign sits at least 2.2 miles from every other callout heard
in the same direction, and from every sign on the 2026-10-01 sheets.

## Ohio State Reformatory, Mansfield (I-71 Exit 176)

100 Reformatory Road, the prison that played Shawshank State Prison in The
Shawshank Redemption; open for tours 10 to 4 seven days a week, run by the
Mansfield Reformatory Preservation Society (Wikipedia; mrps.org). It is west
of I-71 Exit 176 on US 30. The Columbus to Mansfield and Cleveland to
Mansfield legs end in town and read "ahead"; the through Akron to Columbus leg
reads for Exit 176.

## Hopewell Culture National Historical Park, Chillicothe

Mound City Group, 16062 Ohio 104, four miles north of Chillicothe: twenty-five
Hopewell mounds inside an earthen wall, built about 200 BC to AD 500; part of
the Hopewell Ceremonial Earthworks World Heritage Site since 2023. Visitor
center daily 9 to 4, grounds dawn to dusk (National Park Service; Wikipedia,
"Mound City Group"). Signed on the two legs into Chillicothe.

## Newark Earthworks (I-70 Exit 129)

The Great Circle and the Octagon, built by the Hopewell between 100 BC and
AD 400; the Octagon tracks the moon's 18.6-year cycle. A golf course held the
Octagon until January 1, 2025, when the Ohio History Connection took it over;
World Heritage Site since 2023 (Wikipedia, "Newark Earthworks"). North of
Exit 129 on Ohio 79. Eastbound the Hebron callout fills the gap before the
exit, so that sign stands three miles out and reads "ahead".

## The Y-Bridge, Zanesville

The fifth Y-Bridge, opened 1984, spans the meeting of the Licking and
Muskingum Rivers and branches mid-river, so a driver can cross and come off
on the side they started (Wikipedia, "Y-Bridge"). Signed on the two legs into

## Covered bridges of Ashtabula County (I-90)

Ashtabula County has seventeen drivable covered bridges, among them the
Smolen-Gulf Bridge, 613 feet, the longest covered bridge in the United
States, and the West Liberty Covered Bridge in Geneva, the shortest drivable
one (Wikipedia, "Smolen-Gulf Bridge"). The I-90 exits for Ashtabula are
crowded with village callouts, so all four signs read "ahead".
