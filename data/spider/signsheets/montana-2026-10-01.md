# Montana attractions, both directions -- 2026-10-01

Draft for the owner's review, not yet approved.

Thirteen real Montana roadside attractions on Interstates 90, 94 and 15
and US 2 and US 191, each signed in every direction a leg passes it. Every
`leg:` is written the way the driver reads the sign and `at_mi` counts from
that end; `tools/bake_billboards.py` mirrors the milepost onto a leg stored
the other way round and records which way the billboard faces.

Mileposts come from projecting each attraction onto the legs' dense
geometry, read against each leg's interchange list. "Next exit" signs stand
between the attraction's exit and the exit before it; "ahead" signs about
eight to ten miles out, or where river and village callouts crowd an exit.
A sign about a town stands on the legs into it and on the through legs
before it. Each sits at least 2.2 miles from every other callout heard in
the same direction. The Billings to Salt Lake City leg runs I-90 to
Bozeman and then US 191 through the Gallatin Canyon to West Yellowstone, so
its Montana signs are Greycliff, Bozeman and West Yellowstone.

Law: the Montana Outdoor Advertising Act (MCA 75-15) allows off-premise
signs beside the interstate and primary roads in commercial and industrial
areas. Montana's scenic byways (Kings Hill on US 89, Pintler on Montana 1,
St. Regis to Paradise on Montana 135, Lake Koocanusa on Montana 37, and the
Beartooth) are not on these legs.

## Old Montana Prison, Deer Lodge (I-90 Exits 184 and 187)

The territorial prison held its first inmate on July 2, 1871 and was
emptied in the late 1970s; the complex now houses five museums under one
admission, with tours year-round (pcmaf.org). The Missoula to Butte leg
already speaks one of the five, Yesterday's Playthings, at Exit 187; the
prison line is a different bit, and the westbound signs stand 2.3 miles
from that callout.

## Missouri Headwaters State Park, Three Forks (I-90 Exit 278)

Where the Jefferson, Madison and Gallatin rivers meet to form the
Missouri; Lewis and Clark named them in 1805 for President Jefferson,
Secretary of State Madison and Treasury Secretary Gallatin. Three miles off
Exit 278. The fetch of Montana State Parks' page failed this session; these
are long-settled facts (Wikipedia, Missouri River). River and village

Where the Lakota, Cheyenne and Arapaho defeated Custer's Seventh Cavalry

visitor center is under construction and a temporary contact station
serves visitors (National Park Service, hours page, 2026). Exit 509 at
Crow Agency comes first southbound, so every line reads "ahead". A battle

## Pompeys Pillar National Monument (I-94 Exit 23)

A 150-foot sandstone butte by the Yellowstone; William Clark carved his
name and the date, July 25, 1806, the only physical evidence left along the
expedition's route. Bureau of Land Management, interpretive center opened
2006 (Wikipedia).

## Bannack State Park (I-15 Exit 59, then Montana 278 west)

Site of Montana's first major gold strike, July 28, 1862, and its first
territorial capital; more than fifty buildings line Main Street; open all

in 1865.

The spring gives about 150 million gallons a day and feeds the Roe River,

## Grizzly and Wolf Discovery Center, West Yellowstone (US 191 and US 20)

A not-for-profit, AZA-accredited wildlife park beside Yellowstone's west
entrance, open every day of the year; its grizzlies and gray wolves cannot
survive in the wild, and the bears do not hibernate
(grizzlydiscoveryctr.org). The northbound sign stands inside Montana,
before Targhee Pass.
