# Utah attractions, both directions -- 2026-10-01

Draft for the owner's review, not yet approved.

Twelve real Utah roadside attractions on Interstates 15, 70, 80 and 84
and US 40, each signed in every direction a leg passes it. Every `leg:` is
written the way the driver reads the sign and `at_mi` counts from that
end; `tools/bake_billboards.py` mirrors the milepost onto a leg stored the
other way round and records which way the billboard faces.

Mileposts come from projecting each attraction onto the legs' dense
geometry, read against each leg's interchange list. "Next exit" signs stand
between the attraction's exit and the exit before it; "ahead" signs about
four to ten miles out, nearer where the Wasatch Front's village callouts
leave no other gap. Each sits at least 2.2 miles from every other callout
heard in the same direction. Signs on the legs from Las Vegas stand inside
Utah, north of the Arizona line.

Law: the Utah Outdoor Advertising Act allows off-premise signs beside the
interstates in commercial and industrial areas, and Utah Code 72-4-303
puts every designated scenic byway under the federal rule (23 USC 131),
which bars new billboards on them. The Dinosaur Diamond National Scenic
Byway runs US 191 from Blanding through Monticello and Moab to Crescent
Junction, US 6 and 191 from Green River through Price to Helper, and US 40
from Duchesne to Colorado (Utah HB 34, 1998; recreation.gov). Nothing here
stands on those roads, so the Moab, Monticello and Price legs and the US 40
stretch east of Duchesne carry none. The Provo to Fort Collins leg runs up
Provo Canyon on US 189, a road I could not confirm either way this session;
it carries none.

## Golden Spike National Historical Park, Promontory (west of Brigham City)

Where the Central Pacific and Union Pacific met on May 10, 1869; the
ceremony, planned for May 8, the date engraved on the spike, was put off
two days by bad weather and a labor dispute (Wikipedia, Golden spike).
Visitor center open daily early May to late October, Thursday to Monday in
winter; outdoor sites all year (National Park Service). Signed on every
leg through Brigham City, before the city in each direction.

## John Wesley Powell River History Museum, Green River

The river-running museum on Main Street: Powell's expeditions, historic
boats and the River Runners Hall of Fame; open Tuesday to Saturday
(johnwesleypowell.com). Powell, who lost his right arm at Shiloh, led the
1869 boat expedition down the Green and Colorado. Signed on the two I-70
legs into Green River; the US 6 and US 191 approaches are on the Dinosaur
Diamond byway.
