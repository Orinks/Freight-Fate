# Owner decisions on the 2026-10-01 regional drafts

The owner took the drafting agents' recommendations (2026-10-01). These are
the ones that change signs already in the game; the regional sheets carry the
rest. Reasons and sources are in the REVIEW files of the same date.

- Two Pea Soup Andersen's boards stand on officially designated California
  scenic highways, where state law bars billboards: southbound on I-5 and
  eastbound on Highway 152 (REVIEW-west-coast). They go; the other two stay.
- The Goonies sign stands on US 101 at Astoria, the Pacific Coast Scenic
  Byway: it keeps its copy as a roadside landmark callout, facing the way it
  did, like the Keys signs.
- The Bakersfield Sound board said the Crystal Palace "still swings"; it
  closed in August 2025. It now ends on the approved pool wording.
- Cereal City ended on a cereal maker's own slogan; that sentence is cut,
  here and on its new eastbound copy (midwest-reverse-copies).
- Hole N' the Rock and Arches and Canyonlands stand on US 191, the
  Dinosaur Diamond scenic byway, where federal law bars billboards
  (REVIEW-mountain-west): roadside landmark callouts now, same copy.
- The Winslow sign paraphrased the song's lyric; that sentence is cut.

- treatment: billboard
- leg: barstow_ca_us -> bakersfield_ca_us
- at_mi: 126.0
- spoken: Billboard: Bakersfield is home to the Bakersfield Sound. Buck Owens and Merle Haggard built a twangier, harder kind of country music here, a world away from Nashville. Buck Owens' Crystal Palace closed its doors in twenty twenty-five, but the Bakersfield Sound still plays.
