# Georgia attractions, both directions -- draft 2026-10-01

DRAFT for owner review. Nothing here is approved.

9 real attractions, 39 signs.

Each is signed in every direction of travel where a leg passes it, except
where a note says why not. Every `leg:` is written the way the driver
reads the sign and `at_mi` counts from that end; `tools/bake_billboards.py`
mirrors the milepost onto a leg stored the other way round and records which
way the billboard faces.

Billboard law: Georgia permits off-premise boards beside its Interstates and
federal-aid primaries in commercial and industrial areas under the Georgia
Outdoor Advertising Control Act (O.C.G.A. 32-6-70 et seq., GDOT permit),
and I-75 and I-16 are heavily signed. Federal law (23 U.S.C. 131(s)) bars
new boards on designated scenic byways; nothing here stands on one. Rock
City is excluded by standing owner rule (RUTH-CATALOG.md) and Stone
Mountain was left out for its carving.

Mileposts come from projecting each attraction onto the legs' dense route
geometry and from the legs' interchange lists. "Next exit" signs stand
between the attraction's exit and the exit before it; "ahead" signs about
eight to fifteen miles out; a sign naming a town stands on the legs
arriving there. Each sits at least 2.2 miles from every other callout
heard in the same direction, including the other east-south sheets.

## The giant peanut, Ashburn (I-75 Exit 82)

The crowned peanut on its brick tower, put up in 1975 and named an official
state monument in 1998; Hurricane Michael destroyed it in 2018, and a new
peanut was set on the old tower under the old crown by 2023 (Wikipedia,
"Ashburn, Georgia"). Ashburn calls itself the Peanut Capital of the World.
Listed in signage-todo.md.

## The Museum of Aviation, Warner Robins (I-75)

Beside Robins Air Force Base: the second-largest aerospace museum of the Air
Force, free admission, more than 85 aircraft in four buildings on 51
acres, among them the SR-71 Blackbird, which holds the absolute airspeed
record (Wikipedia, "Museum of Aviation (Warner Robins)"). About eight miles
east of I-75.

## Jimmy Carter National Historical Park, Plains (US 280)

The President's hometown: the Plains High School visitor center, the train
depot, the town's oldest building and his 1976 campaign headquarters, the
boyhood farm at Archery, and the garden where he and Rosalynn Carter rest;
open daily (nps.gov/jica). US 280 runs through Plains.

Greenville signs tell the raising; these tell the birth. Signed on the legs
