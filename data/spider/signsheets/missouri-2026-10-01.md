# Missouri attractions, both directions -- 2026-10-01

Draft for the owner's review, not yet approved.

Eight real Missouri roadside attractions, each signed in every direction a leg
passes it where the callouts allow, 39 signs in all, plus two signs reusing
approved copy at the original Lambert's Cafe. Every `leg:` is written the way
the driver reads the sign and `at_mi` counts from that end;
`tools/bake_billboards.py` mirrors the milepost onto a leg stored the other
way round and records which way the billboard faces.

Missouri permits billboards beside its Interstates and primary highways in
commercial and industrial areas under a MoDOT permit (Revised Statutes of
Missouri 226.500 to 226.600). Every sign here stands on an Interstate except
those on the legs into Kansas City, Springfield and Rolla that run US 50,
US 60, US 63, US 65 or Missouri 13 near those cities, none of them a
designated byway; Missouri's byways (Route 66 on its old alignment, the Great
River Road, Crowley's Ridge, the Glacial Hills) carry none.

Mileposts come from projecting each attraction onto the legs and reading the
legs' interchange lists. "Next exit" signs stand between the attraction's exit
and the exit before it; "ahead" signs about six to thirteen miles out. A sign
about a city stands on the legs into it. Each sign sits at least 2.2 miles
from every other callout heard in the same direction, and from every sign on
the 2026-10-01 sheets.

## Harry S. Truman Presidential Library and Museum, Independence (I-70 Exit 12)

500 West US 24, Independence, where Harry and Bess Truman are buried in the
courtyard; the museum reopened in July 2021 after renovation, and The Buck
Stops Here desk sign is on display (Wikipedia; National Archives). Westbound
the Blue River and Lake Tapawingo callouts fill the gap before Exit 12, so
those signs stand nine miles out and read "ahead".

## Arabia Steamboat Museum, Kansas City

400 Grand Boulevard in the River Market, open daily (1856.com). The Arabia
sank in the Missouri in 1856; the river moved, and in 1988 the Hawley family
dug her out from under forty-five feet of silt in a Kansas cornfield, with
jars of pickles still edible (Wikipedia, "Arabia (steamboat)"). Signed on
the legs into Kansas City that stand in Missouri; the I-70 leg from Denver
reaches Missouri only at the river, so it carries none.

## America's National Churchill Museum, Fulton (I-70 Exit 148)

On the Westminster College campus, where Churchill gave the Iron Curtain
speech on March 5, 1946; the Christopher Wren church of St. Mary the Virgin,
Aldermanbury, bombed in London in 1940, was moved to Fulton stone by stone
from 1965 (Wikipedia). About eight miles south of Exit 148 at Kingdom City on
US 54; the Kingdom City callout stands at the exit, so the signs stand two
and a half miles out.

## Pony Express National Museum, St. Joseph

914 Penn Street, in the stables where the Pony Express began in 1860; riders
reached Sacramento in about ten days, and the service lasted eighteen months.

## Ste. Genevieve (I-55 Exit 150)

The first permanent European settlement in Missouri, founded by French
colonists about 1750 and moved to higher ground after the 1785 flood; Ste.
Genevieve National Historical Park keeps its French colonial houses

## George Washington Carver National Monument, Diamond (I-49, Highway V)

5646 Carver Road, the farm where Carver was born; established 1943 as the
first national monument to an African American. From I-49, east on Highway V
