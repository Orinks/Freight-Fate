# East and south: one-way signs given their other side -- draft 2026-10-01

DRAFT for owner review. Copy is not new: every `spoken:` line below is an
existing, approved billboard's text reused VERBATIM, so only the placement
needs a look.

18 real attractions, 32 signs.

Each existing sign here faces one way (its record says `forward` or
`reverse`, or it is a billboard with no `directions`, which faces forward).
Where the other direction of the same road has no sign for that place, and
the same words are true from that side because the sign stands before the
place in that direction, it gets one. Where a second leg runs the same road
past the same place (Atlanta to Dallas on the Atlanta to Birmingham road,
the I-75 legs past Marietta, Mobile to New Orleans on I-10), that leg is
signed too, both ways, with the same words; the review file counts those
separately. A sign whose copy names a town the truck is leaving (Tupelo,
Dothan, Columbus, Asheville) is not mirrored.

Mileposts come from the legs' dense geometry; each sign stands before the
place in its own direction and at least 2.2 miles from every other callout
heard that way, including the other east-south sheets.

Not mirrored, and why: Peach World (Brunswick to Savannah; its operating
status could not be checked), Hershey's Chocolate Town and Amish Country
(Harrisburg to Wilmington; that leg's own route points stray sixteen miles
from its geometry), Smithfield Ham (Norfolk to Petersburg; same fault),
Dinosaur Land (southbound on I-81 its turnoff is inside the first miles out
of Winchester), and the Wytheville pencil, Monticello, Krispy Kreme, the
Coca-Cola inventor and the Dreamland lines, which name the town just left.
