# New York attractions, both directions -- draft 2026-10-01

DRAFT for owner review. Nothing here is approved.

14 real attractions, 63 signs.

Each is signed in every direction of travel where a leg passes it, except
where a note says why not. Every `leg:` is written the way the driver
reads the sign and `at_mi` counts from that end; `tools/bake_billboards.py`
mirrors the milepost onto a leg stored the other way round and records which
way the billboard faces.

Billboard law: New York permits off-premise boards along its Interstates and
primaries under Highway Law section 88. Along the Thruway (I-90, I-87 south
of Albany, I-190) any sign within 660 feet of the right of way needs a
Thruway Authority permit (Public Authorities Law section 361-a; Thruway
form TAP-620), and permitted boards do stand on it, so the Thruway is
signed. The Adirondack and Catskill parks carry none: Environmental
Conservation Law section 9-0305 bars advertising signs inside both parks
without a state permit, the 1924 sign law that removed some 1,400 boards
(protectadks.org). Nothing here stands inside either Blue Line; the
Northway north of Glens Falls carries nothing.

Mileposts come from projecting each attraction's turnoff onto the legs'
dense route geometry (`data/world_data/us/geometry`) and from the legs'
interchange lists. "Next exit" signs stand between the attraction's exit and
the exit before it; "ahead" signs about eight to fifteen miles out. A sign
naming a city stands on the legs arriving there, about ten miles out. Each
sits at least 2.2 miles from every other callout heard in the same
direction (rivers, museums, villages within a mile and a half of the road,
same-facing billboards, and the other signs on this sheet and on the other
east-south sheets of 2026-10-01).

## The Oneida Community Mansion House, Oneida (Thruway Exits 33 and 34)

170 Kenwood Avenue, Oneida: the home of the Oneida Community, a utopian
commune founded in 1848 that owned its property in common; the community
dissolved in 1881 and its business became Oneida Limited, the silverware
company (oneidacommunity.org, including its exhibit "Oneida Ltd.: More Than
a Silverware Company"). Museum open Wednesday to Sunday. It is about six
miles south of the Thruway between Exits 33 and 34, so the copy reads
"ahead in Oneida" rather than naming an exit.
