## Decisions

1. **Oregon carries attraction billboards. Recommend approving the Oregon
   sheet as drafted.** Oregon caps billboards rather than banning them: no
   permit for a new sign since 2007 (ORS 377.725), a new site needs an old
   permit retired or a banked credit, and about 1,700 permitted boards stood
   at the last count I found (Oregon Legislature, 2009). The sheet already keeps out of the Columbia River Gorge National
   Scenic Area (new billboards barred, OAR 350-081-0112), US 101 (Pacific
   Coast Scenic Byway) and the Volcanic Legacy byway. If you would rather
   treat Oregon like Washington's scenic system, the same lines can become
   landmark callouts, as the Keys did.
2. **Two existing California boards stand on officially designated scenic
   highways, where Business and Professions Code 5440.1 bars them. Recommend
   removing both:** "Pea Soup Andersen's (southbound)" on
   `san_francisco_ca_us -> los_angeles_ca_us` (I-5 between I-580 and Route
   152) and "Pea Soup Andersen's (eastbound)" on
   `salinas_ca_us -> fresno_ca_us` (Highway 152 east of the Santa Clara
   County line). The northbound I-5 and westbound 152 boards stay, so
   Santa Nella is still signed from two sides.
3. **"The Goonies" stands on US 101 at Astoria, the Pacific Coast Scenic
   Byway. Recommend converting it to a landmark callout, as with the Keys.**
4. **"The Bakersfield Sound" placed board ends "The Crystal Palace still
   swings", but the Crystal Palace closed in 2025. Recommend changing that
   sentence to the pool's approved wording: "Buck Owens' Crystal Palace
   closed its doors in twenty twenty-five, but the Bakersfield Sound still
   plays."** It is not copied anywhere on these sheets until then.
5. **Three signs stand outside the four states. Recommend keeping them:**
   Maryhill Stonehenge is in Washington but is signed only on I-84 in
   Oregon; the two Wendover Will reverse copies stand on I-80 in Utah, about
   five miles east of the line; the southbound Jackpot sign stands in Idaho.
   Worth checking against the Washington and Utah drafts for spacing.
6. **City sign codes were not checked one by one** (the search budget ran
   out partway through Oregon). Recommend accepting: the city-node signs
   stand on the approaches, mostly eight to twelve miles out.

### Law, by state

- **California.** Billboards allowed beside Interstates and the National
  Highway System with a Caltrans permit (Outdoor Advertising Act, B&P 5200
  and following); barred on classified landscaped freeways (5440) and on
  officially designated scenic highways and byways (5440.1). Designated
  segments on the game's roads, from the Caltrans list of October 2025: I-5
  Route 152 to I-580; Highway 152 east of the Santa Clara County line;
  Highway 156 Castroville to US 101; US 101 at Del Norte Coast Redwoods and
  the Gaviota Coast; US 395 in Mono County and Fort Independence to Fish
  Springs Road; I-580 over the Altamont; I-680 Fremont to Walnut Creek;
  I-280; Highway 12 by Sonoma. US 97 Weed to Oregon is the Volcanic Legacy
  Scenic Byway. No new sign stands on any of them.
- **Oregon.** Motorist Information Act, ORS 377.700 to 377.844: a fixed
  number of permits, none new since 2007. No new billboards in the Gorge
  Scenic Area or on scenic byways (23 USC 131(s)).
- **Nevada.** NRS 410.220 to 410.410 and NDOT permits; no new billboards on
  the state's scenic byways, which on the game's roads are US 93 north of
  Crystal Springs and US 6, 50 and 93 south of Ely to Utah. Hoover Dam is
  left out because both approaches cross Lake Mead National Recreation Area.
- **Idaho.** Idaho Code title 40, chapter 19, with ITD permits; no new signs
  on scenic byways, which here means Highway 55 (Payette River) and the
  Selkirk Loop north and west of Sandpoint.
