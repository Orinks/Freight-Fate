# Oklahoma attractions, both directions -- 2026-10-01

Draft for the owner's review, not yet approved.

Fourteen real Oklahoma roadside attractions on Interstates 40, 44 and 35
and US 64, 69, 75, 177, 287, 400 and 412, each signed in every direction a
leg passes it. Every `leg:` is written the way the driver reads the sign
and `at_mi` counts from that end; `tools/bake_billboards.py` mirrors the
milepost onto a leg stored the other way round and records which way the
billboard faces. Block names read "(from <city>)", the city the driver set
out from on that leg.

Mileposts come from projecting each attraction onto the legs' dense
geometry. Every line here reads "ahead", eight to ten miles out, or four to
five where the Will Rogers Turnpike's village callouts crowd Claremore. A
sign about a town stands on the legs into it. Each sits at least 2.2 miles
from every other callout heard in the same direction. No sign stands in
Texas, Kansas, Missouri, Arkansas or Colorado.

Law: the Oklahoma Highway Advertising Control Act (69 O.S. 1271 and
following) allows off-premise signs beside the interstates, the turnpikes
and the primary roads in commercial and industrial areas. The state's
scenic byways bar new ones. The Osage Nation Heritage Trail is US 60
between Ponca City and Bartlesville, so that leg carries none (no Woolaroc
or Marland sign on it). The Historic Route 66 byway follows the old road
and the I-40 business loops, not the I-40 and I-44 mainlines the signs
stand on. The Cherokee Hills, Wichita Mountains and Talimena byways are not
on these legs; the Wichita Mountains sign stands on I-44, not on OK 49.

## Stafford Air and Space Museum, Weatherford (I-40)

Built around Weatherford native Thomas P. Stafford, who flew four space
missions including Apollo 10 to the Moon; it shows his Gemini VI-A
spacecraft and a Titan II; a Smithsonian Affiliate (Wikipedia).

## Marland Mansion, Ponca City

E. W. Marland's 55-room "Palace on the Prairie," built 1925 to 1928; a
1928 takeover of his oil company by J. P. Morgan Jr. had the family out by
1931; a museum since 1976 (Wikipedia). Signed only on US 177 from
Stillwater; the US 60 approach is the Osage Nation Heritage Trail byway.
