
# East and Central Texas attractions, both directions -- 2026-09-30

Draft for the owner. 43 real roadside attractions on the Interstate 35
corridor (Dallas-Fort Worth, Waco, Austin, San Antonio) and east of it,
south to Corpus Christi, in 209 signs, each attraction signed in every
direction of travel where a leg passes it and a slot is free. Every `leg:`
is written the way the driver reads the sign and `at_mi` counts from that
end; `tools/bake_billboards.py` mirrors the milepost onto a leg stored the
other way round and records which way the billboard faces.

Texas allows commercial billboards along its Interstates and primary
highways under a TxDOT permit (Texas Transportation Code chapter 391, the
state's Highway Beautification Act). Section 391.252 bans them outright on
nineteen scenic stretches; the ones in this region are kept clear of signs
(see the notes). Several cities ban new billboard structures, but the
existing faces along the interstates through them still carry ads.

Mileposts come from the legs' interchange lists (the attraction's exit and
the one before it) and, on the US and state highway legs, which list no
exits, from where the road passes the attraction or its town. "Next exit"
signs stand between the attraction's exit and the exit before it, at least
a mile and a half apart; "ahead" signs about eight to twelve miles out, or
as close as four where callouts leave no room. Each sits at least 2.2 miles
from every other callout heard in the same direction (rivers, museums,
villages within a mile and a half of the road, billboards facing the same
way, and every other sign on this sheet).

Six of these reuse a sign already baked elsewhere, word for word, on legs
and directions that had none: Czech Stop, the Dr Pepper and Magnolia lines
for Waco, Navasota, College Station, and the Paris Eiffel Tower.

## Waco Mammoth National Monument (I-35 Exit 335C northbound, Exit 339 southbound)

6220 Steinbeck Bend Drive, about four miles west of I-35. The National Park Service sends drivers from the south off at Exit 335C and drivers from the north off at Exit 339 (nps.gov/waco/planyourvisit/directions.htm). Found in 1978 by two teenagers, Paul Barron and Eddie Bufkin, hunting arrowheads and fossils; the only known nursery herd of Columbian mammoths (nps.gov/waco history; Wikipedia). It comes last of the Waco signs, so it fills only the slots the others leave.

## Notes for the owner

43 attractions, 209 signs. A dry run of `tools/bake_billboards.py`
on this sheet resolves every leg and bakes every sign (no `--write`). A
spacing check against the baked legs found no callout within 2.2 miles of
any sign in its direction, and no two signs share a name on one leg. Every
new line is spelled out with no digits and runs twenty-one to twenty-six
words; the six reused lines keep their approved length. The jokes are
original.

Billboard law. Texas Transportation Code chapter 391 (the Texas Highway
Beautification Act, run by TxDOT's Right of Way Division) permits commercial
off-premise signs on the Interstate and primary systems under a TxDOT
permit, with size, spacing and lighting rules. Section 391.252(a) bans them
on nineteen listed roads
(https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._transp._code_section_391.252). The
ones in this region, and what this sheet does about them:

- Texas 21 from College Station's south city limits to US 290: no Aggies
  sign on the Austin to College Station approach. The College Station to
  Austin leg leaves Texas 21 for US 290 near Paige; its Southside Market and
  Capitol signs stand on US 290, west of that.
- Any highway in the Sam Houston, Davy Crockett or Sabine National Forests:
  no sign on I-45 between Willis and Huntsville. The northbound Sam Houston
  statue sign stands between Exits 109 and 112, north of Huntsville State
  Park. That is the one placement worth checking against the forest
  boundary before baking.
- US 77 between Texas 186 and Texas 44 (Raymondville to Robstown): the
  Brownsville to Corpus Christi leg's USS Lexington sign stands eight miles
  north of Robstown, past Texas 44.
- US 290 from Austin's west city limits to Fredericksburg: no Capitol sign
  on the Kerrville to Austin leg.
- Texas 105 Sour Lake to Cleveland, Texas 130 segments 1 to 4, and the
  others: no sign on them (the Beaumont to College Station leg runs US 90
  and Texas 99, not Texas 105).

Several Texas cities, Houston and Austin among them, ban new billboard
structures within city limits, but grandfathered faces still line the
interstates through them, so signs there are true to the road.
(Background: Community Impact on the 2016 Auspro ruling and the 2017 fix;
capitol.texas.gov bill analyses.)
