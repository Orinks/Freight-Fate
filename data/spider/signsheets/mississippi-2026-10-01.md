# Mississippi attractions, both directions -- draft 2026-10-01

DRAFT for owner review. Nothing here is approved.

7 real attractions, 27 signs.

Each is signed in every direction of travel where a leg passes it, except
where a note says why not. Every `leg:` is written the way the driver
reads the sign and `at_mi` counts from that end; `tools/bake_billboards.py`
mirrors the milepost onto a leg stored the other way round and records which
way the billboard faces.

Billboard law: Mississippi permits off-premise boards beside its Interstates
and federal-aid primaries in commercial and industrial areas under its
outdoor advertising law (Miss. Code 49-23-1 et seq., MDOT permit). Federal
law (23 U.S.C. 131(s)) bars new boards on designated scenic byways, and the
Natchez Trace Parkway, a national parkway, carries none; nothing here
stands on it. The signs stand on I-20, I-59, I-10 and on US 61 and US 82,
where boards stand.

Mileposts come from projecting each attraction onto the legs' dense route
geometry and from the legs' interchange lists. "Next exit" signs stand
between the attraction's exit and the exit before it; "ahead" signs about
eight to fifteen miles out; a sign naming a town stands on the legs
arriving there. Each sits at least 2.2 miles from every other callout
heard in the same direction, including the other east-south sheets.

## Vicksburg National Military Park (I-20 Exit 4B)

The 1863 siege, forty-seven days: a sixteen-mile tour road past more than
1,400 monuments, the national cemetery, and the USS Cairo, a Union gunboat
sunk in the Yazoo in 1862 and raised a century later; 3201 Clay Street, at
I-20 Exit 4B (nps.gov/vick). A history site, so the line carries no joke.
Signed on I-20 both ways and on the legs arriving in Vicksburg.

## The B.B. King Museum and Delta Interpretive Center, Indianola (US 82)

Opened September 13, 2008, around the restored brick cotton gin where King
worked in the 1940s; he was buried in its memorial garden in 2015
(Wikipedia, "B.B. King Museum and Delta Interpretive Center"). He named his
guitars Lucille. Written "B B King" for the screen reader. On US 82 through
Indianola, Greenville to Grenada.
