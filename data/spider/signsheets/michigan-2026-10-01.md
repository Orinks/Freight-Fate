# Michigan attractions, both directions -- 2026-10-01

Draft for the owner's review, not yet approved.

Nine real Michigan roadside attractions, each signed in every direction a leg
passes it where the law and the callouts allow, 40 signs in all. Every `leg:`
is written the way the driver reads the sign and `at_mi` counts from that end;
`tools/bake_billboards.py` mirrors the milepost onto a leg stored the other
way round and records which way the billboard faces.

Michigan permits billboards beside its Interstates and primary highways in
business areas under an MDOT permit (Highway Advertising Act of 1972, renamed
the Scenic Michigan Act), and bars any sign on a Michigan Heritage Route, now
called a Pure Michigan Byway (SFA analysis of SB 445, 1997). Those include
I-94 from the Indiana line to M-63 at St. Joseph and I-196 from there to
Holland (the West Michigan Pike), I-69 from the Indiana line to the
Calhoun-Eaton county line, US 31 from Holland to Ludington, US 2 in Iron
County, the UP Hidden Coast trail through Escanaba, and US 41 on the
Keweenaw (michiganhighways.org; Wikipedia, "Pure Michigan Byway"). No sign
here stands on one of them.

Mileposts come from the legs' interchange lists. The Detroit end of I-94 has
sparse geometry and no Exit 206 (Oakwood Boulevard) in its list, so the Henry
Ford and the giant tire are placed from Exit 206 and the tire's real position
(between the Southfield Freeway and Outer Drive), estimated at 12.3 and 14.3
miles from Detroit. Each sign sits at least 2.2 miles from every other callout
heard in the same direction, and from every sign on the 2026-10-01 sheets.

## The Henry Ford, Dearborn (I-94 Exit 206)

The Henry Ford Museum of American Innovation, 20900 Oakwood Boulevard, open
daily 9:30 to 5: the bus where Rosa Parks was arrested, the chair from Ford's
Theatre where Lincoln was shot, and presidential limousines including
Kennedy's (Wikipedia; thehenryford.org). Westbound out of Detroit the signs
read "ahead"; eastbound "next exit", past the Southfield Freeway.

## Uniroyal Giant Tire, Allen Park (I-94)

Eighty feet across and twelve tons, built as a Ferris wheel for the 1964-65
New York World's Fair and standing beside I-94 in Allen Park since 1966,
between the Southfield Freeway and the Outer Drive overpass (Wikipedia,
"Uniroyal Giant Tire"). Westbound only: eastbound the leg's Redford and
Livonia village callouts fill the two miles before it.

## The Soo Locks, Sault Ste. Marie

The locks lift ships the twenty-one feet between Lake Superior and Lake Huron;
the Poe Lock takes the thousand-foot lake freighters. The Army Corps of
Engineers runs a visitor center and a free viewing deck; the locks close for
winter from mid-January to late March (Wikipedia, "Soo Locks"). Signed on the
two legs into the city.

## Pictured Rocks National Lakeshore, Munising (M-28)

The first national lakeshore, 1966: sandstone cliffs up to two hundred feet
over Lake Superior, streaked red by iron, black by manganese and green by
copper; open all year (Wikipedia; National Park Service). M-28 is a Lake
Superior Circle Tour route, a tourist route and not a Heritage Route.

## River Raisin National Battlefield Park, Monroe (I-75)

cry (Wikipedia; National Park Service). The Frenchtown callout fills the gap
before Exit 15 southbound, and the leg's exit list is out of order
