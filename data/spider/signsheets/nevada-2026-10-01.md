# Nevada attractions, both directions -- 2026-10-01

Draft for the owner's review, not yet approved.

Thirteen real roadside attractions in Nevada, each signed in every direction
a leg passes it, 50 signs in all, plus two sections that carry approved copy
word for word onto other roads into Fallon and Ely (5 signs). Every `leg:` is
written the way the driver reads the sign and `at_mi` counts from that end;
`tools/bake_billboards.py` mirrors the milepost onto a leg stored the other
way round and records which way the billboard faces.

Nevada allows billboards beside its Interstates and the National Highway
System under an NDOT permit, off the state right-of-way (NRS 410.220 to
410.410; dot.nv.gov, Signs and Billboards). New billboards are barred on its
designated scenic byways (23 USC 131(s)); on the game's roads those are US 93,
the Great Basin Highway, from the State Route 318 junction at Crystal Springs
north to Majors Place, and US 6, 50 and 93 together from south of Ely to Utah
(Nevada Scenic Byways list). Nothing here stands on either. The Valley of Fire
road and the Las Vegas Strip are byways too, but no sign stands on them;
the Valley of Fire signs are on I-15. Hoover Dam is left out: both approaches
run through Lake Mead National Recreation Area, federal land with no
roadside billboards.

Mileposts come from projecting each attraction onto the legs' dense geometry,
read against each leg's interchange list. "Next exit" signs stand between the
attraction's exit and the exit before it, one to two miles out; "ahead" signs
about eight to twelve miles out. A sign about a city stands on the legs into
it, never on the ones leaving. Each sign sits at least 2.2 miles from every
other callout heard in the same direction and every other sign on the
west-coast sheets.
