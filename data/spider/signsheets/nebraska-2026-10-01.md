# Nebraska attractions, both directions -- 2026-10-01

Draft for the owner's review, not yet approved.

Ten real Nebraska roadside attractions, nine on Interstate 80 and one on
US 77, each signed in every direction a leg passes it. Interstate 80
eastbound had no attraction sign at all across the state; every attraction
here faces both ways, and the reverse-copies sheet adds the eastbound side
of the four older westbound signs (the Archway, Gothenburg's Pony Express
station, the Golden Spike Tower and Cabela's). Every `leg:` is written the
way the driver reads the sign and `at_mi` counts from that end;
`tools/bake_billboards.py` mirrors the milepost onto a leg stored the other
way round and records which way the billboard faces.

Mileposts come from projecting each attraction onto the legs' dense
geometry, read against each leg's interchange list (the Omaha to Cheyenne
leg lists few exits west of Lincoln, so its exits are projected from the
shorter legs over the same road). "Next exit" signs stand between the
attraction's exit and the exit before it, one to two miles out, a little
more where a village or river callout sits at the exit; "ahead" signs eight
to fifteen miles out. Each sits at least 2.2 miles from every other callout
heard in the same direction.

Law: Nebraska's Highway Beautification Act allows off-premise signs beside
Interstate 80 in commercial and industrial areas. Section 39-218 bars them
along the state's nine scenic byways, so nothing here stands on the Lincoln
Highway (US 30), the Gold Rush Byway (US 385) or the Western Trails Byway
(US 26); the Columbus, Scottsbluff and Sidney US-route legs carry none.

## Robert Henri Museum, Cozad (I-80 Exit 222)

The painter was born Robert Henry Cozad; his father founded the town, shot
a rancher in 1882, and the family fled to Denver under new names, the son
becoming Robert Earl Henri (Wikipedia, Robert Henri; Crystal Bridges). The
museum, a mile off the interstate, holds the largest collection of his work
on display and is open March through mid-December (roberthenrimuseum.org).
Westbound on the Kearney leg the sign stands 2.8 miles out because the
leg's One Hundredth Meridian Museum callout sits half a mile before the
exit.

## Homestead National Historical Park, Beatrice (US 77, then Nebraska 4)

Four miles west of Beatrice on land that includes some of the first acres
claimed under the Homestead Act; Daniel Freeman filed his claim at 12:10
a.m. on January 1, 1863, the day the act took effect; entry is free
(Wikipedia; National Park Service).
