# Arizona attractions, both directions -- 2026-10-01

Draft for the owner's review, not yet approved.

Nineteen real Arizona roadside attractions on Interstates 40, 17, 10, 19
and 8 and US 89, US 95, AZ 87, AZ 90 and AZ 95, each signed in every
direction a leg passes it. The 466-mile Albuquerque to Phoenix leg had no
attraction sign; it now carries Petrified Forest, Walnut Canyon, Flagstaff's
observatory and Montezuma Castle both ways, and the reverse-copies sheet
puts the older Route 66 signs (Meteor Crater, Two Guns, the Jack Rabbit,
the Wigwam Motel) on it too. Every `leg:` is written the way the driver
reads the sign and `at_mi` counts from that end; `tools/bake_billboards.py`
mirrors the milepost onto a leg stored the other way round and records
which way the billboard faces. Block names read "(from <city>)", the city
the driver set out from on that leg.

Mileposts come from projecting each attraction onto the legs' dense
geometry, read against each leg's interchange list. "Next exit" signs stand
between the attraction's exit and the exit before it; "ahead" signs about
five to ten miles out. Each sits at least 2.2 miles from every other callout
heard in the same direction. No sign stands in California or Nevada.

Law: Arizona's outdoor advertising statutes (ARS 28-7901 and following)
allow off-premise signs beside the interstates and primary roads in
commercial and industrial areas. ADOT's designated parkways, historic and
scenic roads (ADOT list, 2008) cover no interstate mainline; the ones on
these legs are US 89A from Mingus Mountain through Jerome, Cottonwood, Dry
Creek and Oak Creek Canyon, US 60's Gila-Pinal Scenic Road (mileposts 214.5
to 240.5), US 93's Joshua Forest Scenic Road (126.5 to 180), and AZ 82's
Patagonia-Sonoita Scenic Road. None of these carries a sign here, so the
Prescott to Flagstaff and Cottonwood to Sedona legs carry none, and Sedona
itself bans off-premise signs. US 89 north of Flagstaff and I-40 near
Lupton cross the Navajo Nation, whose own sign rules apply; billboards
stand along both today.

## Petrified Forest National Park (I-40 Exit 311)

I-40 crosses the park; the Painted Desert entrance is Exit 311. The wood is
Late Triassic, about 225 million years old (Wikipedia). Visitors who take
pieces have mailed them back for decades with letters blaming bad luck,
which the park keeps on display; that is long-documented park lore, not
re-checked this session.
