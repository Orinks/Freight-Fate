# Iowa attractions, both directions -- 2026-10-01

Draft for the owner's review, not yet approved.

Ten real Iowa roadside attractions, each signed in every direction a leg
passes it where the callouts allow, 30 signs in all. Every `leg:` is written
the way the driver reads the sign and `at_mi` counts from that end;
`tools/bake_billboards.py` mirrors the milepost onto a leg stored the other
way round and records which way the billboard faces.

Iowa permits billboards beside its Interstates and primary highways in
commercial and industrial areas under an Iowa DOT permit (Iowa Code chapter
306C) and prohibits new ones along any state or nationally designated scenic
byway (Iowa Code 306C.11 and 306D.4; Iowa DOT). Nothing here stands on the
Lincoln Highway Heritage Byway (US 30), the Great River Road, the Loess
Hills, or the state byways around Amana and Winterset; those signs stand on
I-80 and I-35. The Iowa 80 truck stop already has its own pool line.

Mileposts come from projecting each attraction onto the legs and reading the
legs' interchange lists. "Next exit" signs stand between the attraction's exit
and the exit before it, one to three miles out where village callouts allow;
"ahead" signs about six to ten miles out. A sign about a city stands on the
legs into it. Each sign sits at least 2.2 miles from every other callout heard
in the same direction, and from every sign on the 2026-10-01 sheets.

## The Amana Colonies (I-80 Exit 225)

Seven villages founded from 1855 by German Pietists of the Community of True
Inspiration, communal until 1932; restaurants serve family-style, and Amana
Refrigeration began here in 1934 (Wikipedia, "Amana Colonies"). North of Exit
225 on US 151; the Iowa Valley Scenic Byway runs through the villages, not on
I-80.

## Herbert Hoover National Historic Site and Presidential Library, West Branch (I-80 Exit 254)

The birthplace cottage, his father's blacksmith shop, the graves of Herbert
and Lou Henry Hoover, and the presidential library and museum, at Exit 254

the first president born west of the Mississippi. The West Branch village and
museum callouts sit just before the exit, so the signs stand two and a half to

## The Surf Ballroom, Clear Lake (I-35 Exit 194)

## Sergeant Floyd Monument, Sioux City (I-29)

A hundred-foot sandstone obelisk on Floyd's Bluff over the Missouri, dedicated
1901, the first National Historic Landmark (1960); Charles Floyd was the only

## Snake Alley, Burlington

Built 1894, 275 feet with seven turns down a 21 percent grade; Ripley called it
the crookedest street in the world, and it is one-way downhill because horses
could not manage the climb (Wikipedia, "Snake Alley (Burlington, Iowa)").
Signed on the two legs into Burlington, west of the Great River Road.
