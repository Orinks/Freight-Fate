Drafts only; nothing is baked.

- Attractions: 84 new (Illinois 15, Indiana 14, Ohio 10, Michigan 8, Iowa 10,

- Signs: 383 in all. 334 carry new copy (Illinois 67, Indiana 60, Ohio 34,

- Reused-copy signs: 49, approved copy word for word: 47 in

## Decisions

1. **Existing billboards on national scenic byways. Recommend converting them
   to landmark callouts, as the Keys were on 2026-09-30.** Butcher Hollow and
   Hatfield and McCoy Country stand on US 23 in Kentucky, the Country Music
   Highway, a National Scenic Byway; Grant's Galena stands on US 20 between
   East Dubuque and Galena, the Great River Road. The remove-and-landmark
   blocks are drafted, same words minus "Billboard:", in
   `byway-conversions-midwest-2026-10-01.md`. The new westbound Galena sign
   stands east of Galena, off the byway.
2. **Cereal City ends on Kellogg's own slogan. Recommend cutting "Tony the
   Tiger says it is grreat." from the original and from its new eastbound
   copy** (the copy rule bars real ad text). The eastbound copy is drafted
   verbatim for now.
3. **USS LST-325 leaves Evansville every September for a three-to-four-week
   river trip (2026: from September 19). Recommend keeping its seven signs;**
   the copy does not promise she is in port.
4. **The Ark Encounter is a faith attraction. Recommend keeping it** with the
   neutral copy drafted ("They picked high ground.").
5. **Lambert's at Sikeston reuses the approved Ozark "Throwed Rolls" line,
   which names no town. Recommend keeping it** at the original restaurant.
6. **Billboard law, per state. Recommend approving as drafted:** no sign stands
   on a designated byway.
   - Illinois: allowed in business areas under the Highway Advertising Control
     Act (225 ILCS 440). Byways (Route 66, Great River Road, Illinois River
     Road, Lincoln Highway, National Road) are not used; Illinois 29 into
     Peoria is left unsigned.
   - Indiana: allowed in commercial and industrial areas (IC 8-23-20). The
     Lincoln Highway byway (US 30 and US 33), the Michigan Road (US 31 north
     of Rochester), the National Road, Historic Pathways (US 50 and US 150 at
     Vincennes) and the Ohio River byway are not used.
   - Ohio: allowed in commercial and industrial areas (ORC 5516). The six
     national byways are not used; US 24 along the Maumee Valley byway is
     left unsigned.
   - Michigan: the Scenic Michigan Act bars any sign on a Heritage Route
     (Pure Michigan Byway): I-94 Indiana line to St. Joseph, I-196 to Holland,
     US 31 Holland to Ludington, I-69 Indiana line to the Calhoun-Eaton line,
     US 2 in Iron County, Escanaba, the Keweenaw. None used; Holland's windmill
     is not signed for that reason.
   - Iowa: Iowa Code 306C.11 and 306D.4 prohibit new devices on any state or
     national scenic byway. US 30 (Lincoln Highway), the Great River Road and
     the Loess Hills are not used; Burlington's Snake Alley signs stand west of
     the Great River Road.
   - Missouri: allowed in commercial and industrial areas (RSMo 226.500 to
     226.600); every sign is on an Interstate or a US route near a city, none
     a byway.
   - Kentucky: KRS 177.830 to 177.890 and 603 KAR 10:040; no billboard on a
     designated scenic byway. US 23, US 231 Hartford to Beaver Dam, US 60
     around Owensboro and Henderson, and the Woodlands Trace are not used.
   - West Virginia: permitted under WV Code 17-22; the national byways and the
     state byways (US 50 Northwestern Turnpike among them) are not used. The
     New River Gorge Bridge gets no southbound copy because US 19 south of it
     runs beside the Coal Heritage Trail.
7. **Found along the way in the leg data, not billboards. Recommend a data
   fix pass recorded under Found along the way:** spoken river crossings and a
   state line are miles off on several legs.
   - St. Louis to Nashville (I-24): "Crossing the Ohio River" at mile 155, about
     fifteen miles before the real crossing at the Kentucky line.
   - Indianapolis to Nashville and Columbus to Louisville (I-65): "Crossing the
     Ohio River" near Sellersburg, eight to eleven miles north of the river.
   - Chicago to St. Louis (I-55): "Crossing the Mississippi River" between
     Exits 9 and 6 in Illinois. Chicago to Des Moines: the Mississippi callout
     about eight miles east of the Le Claire bridge. Galesburg to Davenport: the
     Rock River and the Mississippi announced at the same spot.
   - Minneapolis to Des Moines (I-35): the Minnesota-Iowa line recorded at mile
     153.6; the real line is near mile 113, so Clear Lake reads as Minnesota.
   - Indianapolis to Nashville (I-65): Kentucky exit numbers out of order
     (Cave City listed as 112, Park City as 121, Horse Cave as 117).

### Illinois

2. Billboard: The Illinois Route Sixty-Six Hall of Fame and Museum, next exit in Pontiac. Admission is free. The old road never charged anybody to remember it.

Lincoln, Illinois

5. Billboard: The Abraham Lincoln Presidential Museum, ahead in Springfield. Inside are a log cabin and the front of the White House, his whole trip under one roof.

The Ariston Cafe, Litchfield

Cahokia Mounds State Historic Site, Collinsville

7. Billboard: Cahokia Mounds, ahead. A thousand years ago a city bigger than London stood here, and its great mound rose one basket of earth at a time.
8. Billboard: Cahokia Mounds, next exit. A thousand years ago a city bigger than London stood here, and its great mound rose one basket of earth at a time.

Starved Rock State Park, Utica

9. Billboard: Starved Rock State Park, next exit, three miles south. Eighteen sandstone canyons and spring waterfalls. Illinois is not as flat as it lets on.
10. Billboard: Starved Rock State Park, ahead off Route seventy-one. Eighteen sandstone canyons and spring waterfalls. Illinois is not as flat as it lets on.

Superman, Metropolis

John Deere Pavilion, Moline

12. Billboard: The John Deere Pavilion, ahead in Moline. Free admission, and you may climb up into the combines. Nobody will ask you to plow anything.

Caterpillar Visitors Center, Peoria

13. Billboard: The Caterpillar Visitors Center, ahead in Peoria. The tour starts in the bed of a mining truck two and a half stories tall. Try not to compare.

Burpee Museum of Natural History, Rockford

Rochelle Railroad Park

15. Billboard: Rochelle Railroad Park, next exit. Two railroads cross here, and dozens of trains a day pass a free viewing area. Watch somebody else haul for once.

Ronald Reagan Boyhood Home, Dixon

16. Billboard: Ronald Reagan's boyhood home, next exit in Dixon. As a lifeguard on the Rock River he saved seventy-seven swimmers, and once dove for a man's false teeth.

Carl Sandburg State Historic Site, Galesburg

17. Billboard: Carl Sandburg's birthplace, ahead in Galesburg. The poet's three-room cottage still stands, and he rests out back under a boulder named for his only novel.
