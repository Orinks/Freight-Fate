# Wyoming attractions, both directions -- 2026-10-01

Draft for the owner's review, not yet approved.

Eleven real Wyoming roadside attractions on Interstates 80, 25 and 90, each
signed in every direction a leg passes it. Interstate 25 from Cheyenne to
the Montana line had no attraction sign at all. Every `leg:` is written the
way the driver reads the sign and `at_mi` counts from that end;
`tools/bake_billboards.py` mirrors the milepost onto a leg stored the other
way round and records which way the billboard faces.

Mileposts come from projecting each attraction onto the legs' dense
geometry, read against each leg's interchange list (exits missing from a
long leg's list are projected from the shorter legs over the same road).
"Next exit" signs stand between the attraction's exit and the exit before
it; "ahead" signs about eight to ten miles out. A sign about a town stands
on the legs into it and on the through legs before it. Each sits at least
2.2 miles from every other callout heard in the same direction.

Law: Wyoming's outdoor advertising act (W.S. 24-10-101 and following)
allows off-premise signs within 660 feet of the interstates and primary
roads in zoned or unzoned commercial and industrial areas. The state's
scenic byways are mountain and canyon roads (Beartooth, Chief Joseph, Wind
River Canyon, Big Horn, Cloud Peak Skyway, Snowy Range, Centennial, Flaming
Gorge); none is on a leg here.

## Bear River State Park, Evanston (I-80 Exit 6)

A day-use park south of Exit 6 with small herds of bison and elk kept for
viewing; only bull elk are kept, to stop fighting and calves (Wyoming State
Parks, "About Bear River"). The leg's Bear River callout sits between Exits
10 and 6 westbound, so every sign reads "ahead".

## Fort Bridger State Historic Site (I-80 Exit 34)

Jim Bridger and Louis Vasquez opened a trading post here in 1843 to supply
emigrants; later a Mormon outpost and an army fort. Grounds open all year,

## Fort Phil Kearny State Historic Site (I-90 Exit 44)

The Bozeman Trail fort at the center of Red Cloud's War, 1866 to 1868; the
Lakota, Northern Cheyenne and Arapaho closed the trail, the 1868 Fort
Laramie treaty gave up the forts, and the army left. Three miles west of
Exit 44; grounds open all year, the interpretive center May through October
(Wyoming State Parks). A battle site, so the line carries no joke.
