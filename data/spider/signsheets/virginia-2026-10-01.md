# Virginia attractions, both directions -- draft 2026-10-01

DRAFT for owner review. Nothing here is approved.

13 real attractions, 47 signs.

Each is signed in every direction of travel where a leg passes it, except
where a note says why not. Every `leg:` is written the way the driver
reads the sign and `at_mi` counts from that end; `tools/bake_billboards.py`
mirrors the milepost onto a leg stored the other way round and records which
way the billboard faces. One block, the National D-Day Memorial at Bedford,
is a respectful `landmark` callout heard both ways, not a billboard.

Billboard law: Virginia permits off-premise boards beside its Interstates
and federal-aid primaries under Code of Virginia title 33.2, chapter 12
(VDOT outdoor advertising permit), but section 33.2-1216 bars any new board
beside an Interstate, federal-aid primary or National Highway System road
designated a Virginia Byway or scenic highway. So nothing here stands on a
byway: not US 211 into Luray (a Virginia Byway; the Luray Caverns signs
stand on I-81), not US 15, not Business US 13 on the Eastern Shore (a
VDOT scenic road; the Chincoteague signs stand on mainline US 13), and
nothing on Skyline Drive or the Blue Ridge Parkway, which carry none. The
signs stand on I-81, I-64, I-95, US 13, US 220 and US 460, where boards
stand today.

Mileposts come from projecting each attraction's turnoff onto the legs'
dense route geometry and from the legs' interchange lists. "Next exit"
signs stand between the attraction's exit and the exit before it; "ahead"
signs about eight to fifteen miles out; a sign naming a town stands on the
legs arriving there. Each sits at least 2.2 miles from every other callout
heard in the same direction, including the other east-south sheets.

## Natural Bridge State Park, Natural Bridge (I-81 Exits 175 and 180)

The 215-foot limestone arch that carries US 11 over Cedar Creek. Thomas
Jefferson bought it and 157 acres from King George III on July 5, 1774,
for twenty shillings; it became Virginia's 37th state park (WDBJ7, July
2024; gribblenation.org). Reached by US 11 from I-81 Exit 175 or 180.

## Luray Caverns, Luray (I-81 Exit 264, New Market)

The largest cave system in the eastern United States, home of the Great
Stalacpipe Organ (1957), whose keys strike thirty-seven stalactites spread
over three and a half acres; Guinness lists it as the world's largest
musical instrument (NPR, November 2023; zmescience.com). About fourteen
miles east of I-81 Exit 264 on US 211, a Virginia Byway, so the signs
stand on I-81 only.

forty cents or its worth in produce, and four in five patrons paid in

## The National D-Day Memorial, Bedford (US 460)

Bedford lost nineteen men in the first minutes of the Omaha Beach landing
and one more that day, the highest per-capita loss of any American
community on D-Day; the memorial opened in 2001 (WSET; Stars and Stripes,
2024). A respectful `landmark` callout, heard both ways, on the Roanoke to
Lynchburg leg. The owner may prefer to leave it out.

### The National D-Day Memorial

- at_mi: 28.9
- facing: both
- spoken: You are passing Bedford, home of the National D-Day Memorial. This small town lost more of its sons per capita on D-Day than any other in America.
