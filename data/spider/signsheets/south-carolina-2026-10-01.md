# South Carolina attractions, both directions -- draft 2026-10-01

DRAFT for owner review. Nothing here is approved.

8 real attractions, 32 signs.

Each is signed in every direction of travel where a leg passes it, except
where a note says why not. Every `leg:` is written the way the driver
reads the sign and `at_mi` counts from that end; `tools/bake_billboards.py`
mirrors the milepost onto a leg stored the other way round and records which
way the billboard faces.

Billboard law: South Carolina permits off-premise boards beside its
Interstates and federal-aid primaries in commercial and industrial areas
under the Highway Advertising Control Act (S.C. Code 57-25-110 et seq.,
SCDOT permit), and its Interstates are among the most heavily signed in the
country (South of the Border's run is the proof). Federal law (23 U.S.C.
131(s)) bars new boards on designated scenic byways; nothing here stands on
the Cherokee Foothills Scenic Highway (SC 11), the Ashley River Road (SC
61) or the state's other byways. The signs stand on I-85, I-95, I-26, I-77,
I-20, US 17, US 76 and US 378, where boards stand.

Mileposts come from projecting each attraction onto the legs' dense route
geometry and from the legs' interchange lists. "Next exit" signs stand
between the attraction's exit and the exit before it; "ahead" signs about
eight to fifteen miles out; a sign naming a town stands on the legs
arriving there. Each sits at least 2.2 miles from every other callout
heard in the same direction, including the other east-south sheets.

## The Peachoid, Gaffney (I-85 between Exits 90 and 92)

The peach-shaped water tower beside I-85 off Peachoid Road, 135 feet tall
and holding a million gallons, built in 1981 by Chicago Bridge and Iron;
Cherokee County then grew more peaches than all of Georgia, and the tower
had a part in House of Cards (Wikipedia, "Peachoid"). Listed as a
candidate in signage-todo.md. Signed "ahead" before the truck passes it,
both ways.

## Brookgreen Gardens, Murrells Inlet (US 17)

The country's first public sculpture garden, opened in 1932 by Archer and
Anna Hyatt Huntington on four former rice plantations: about 1,445 works,
the world's largest outdoor collection of American figurative sculpture,
and the Lowcountry Zoo (Wikipedia, "Brookgreen Gardens"). On US 17 south of
Murrells Inlet.
