# Alabama attractions, both directions -- draft 2026-10-01

DRAFT for owner review. Nothing here is approved.

9 real attractions, 40 signs.

Each is signed in every direction of travel where a leg passes it, except
where a note says why not. Every `leg:` is written the way the driver
reads the sign and `at_mi` counts from that end; `tools/bake_billboards.py`
mirrors the milepost onto a leg stored the other way round and records which
way the billboard faces.

Billboard law: Alabama permits off-premise boards beside its Interstates and
federal-aid primaries in commercial and industrial areas under the Alabama
Highway Beautification Act (Code of Alabama 23-1-270 et seq., ALDOT
permit), and I-65 and I-20 are heavily signed. Federal law (23 U.S.C.
131(s)) bars new boards on designated scenic byways; nothing here stands on
one (the Natchez Trace Parkway's Alabama corner carries none).

Mileposts come from projecting each attraction onto the legs' dense route
geometry and from the legs' interchange lists. "Next exit" signs stand
between the attraction's exit and the exit before it; "ahead" signs about
eight to fifteen miles out; a sign naming a town stands on the legs
arriving there. Each sits at least 2.2 miles from every other callout
heard in the same direction, including the other east-south sheets.

## The Ave Maria Grotto, Cullman (I-65 Exit 308)

On the grounds of St. Bernard Abbey: 125 miniature reproductions of famous
religious buildings, built over about fifty years by Brother Joseph Zoettl,
a Benedictine monk, from bricks, marbles, shells, costume jewelry, toilet
floats and cold cream jars; open to visitors (Wikipedia, "Ave Maria
Grotto"). About three miles east of I-65 Exit 308 on US 278.

## Vulcan Park, Birmingham

largest cast iron statue in the world, on Red Mountain since 1939; its
bare backside faces the suburb of Homewood, a long-running local joke

## The Clanton peach water tower, Clanton (I-65)

Chilton County grows more than eighty percent of Alabama's peaches, and
Clanton put up a peach-shaped water tower beside I-65 in 1993 (Wikipedia,
"Clanton, Alabama"), a half-million-gallon copy by the builder of the
million-gallon Peachoid in Gaffney (Wikipedia, "Peachoid"), which the South
Carolina sheet signs.
